# Spanish Adaptation Guide

## Required workflow

**Research → English draft → factual review → stable English chapter → Spanish adaptation**

English is canonical. Do not independently research or invent a second factual account in Spanish. When English facts change, reopen the Spanish adaptation and its review status.

## Adapt, do not transpose

Write clear, neutral, natural Spanish for an educated adult native speaker. Preserve meaning, qualification, sourcing, dates, disclaimers, and uncertainty; restructure sentences and examples when that improves comprehension. Do not bulk-translate unstable placeholders.

Virginia training and employment operate primarily in English, so introduce important professional terms bilingually: **Causa probable — Probable cause**, **Sospecha razonable — Reasonable suspicion**, **Registro e incautación — Search and seizure**, and similarly for arrest, detention, warrant, use of force, dispatch, traffic stop, defensive tactics, chain of custody, field training officer, de-escalation, patrol, evidence, citation, and summons.

Retain official names such as **James City County Police Department** and **Hampton Roads Criminal Justice Training Academy**, adding Spanish explanation where helpful. Do not translate a legal term as though the Spanish phrase were an independently controlling Virginia term.

## Review gates

The adapter records the English source revision, checks all citations against shared records, checks terminology consistency, checks accents/Unicode in the PDF, and obtains factual review for any adaptation that changes nuance. A Spanish placeholder must remain unmistakably labeled until these gates pass.
