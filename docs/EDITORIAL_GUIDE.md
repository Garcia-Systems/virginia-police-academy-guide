# Editorial Guide

## Voice and purpose

Write calm, practical, respectful, readable, evidence-based nonfiction for an intelligent adult considering police work. Be supportive without minimizing difficulty. Avoid macho language, clichés, sensationalism, fear, and action-movie framing. Explain rather than coach for an exam.

## Accuracy labels

Every claim must be framed as one of six types:

1. Virginia law or regulation;
2. DCJS compulsory minimum training standards;
3. information HRCJTA itself publishes;
4. information James City County or its Police Department publishes;
5. a generally accepted law-enforcement training concept; or
6. this book's illustrative/editorial organization.

Never imply that an editorial week title is HRCJTA's schedule. Never invent policies, scores, standards, procedures, laws, cases, certification steps, tests, or graduation rules. Say when information could not be verified.

For time-sensitive rules, state the effective date. Use labeled callouts—**Current requirement** and **Future requirement**—when an adopted future rule differs, and give the future effective date.

## Safety boundary

Firearms, defensive tactics, driving, searches, arrests, and force are discussed through law, safety, judgment, accountability, de-escalation, policy, ethics, and the need for supervised professional training. Do not provide operational instructions for harming, evading, overpowering, or targeting people.

## Chapter contract

Use the templates in `templates/`. Narrative openings may be illustrative but may not fabricate an HRCJTA event. “May” descriptions must be grounded in cited, accepted concepts and cannot smuggle in academy-specific assertions. Courthouse experience can provide vocabulary and system context; it never substitutes for police education or experience. Reflection questions are not quizzes.

Every substantive chapter ends with **Official Sources**, **Further Reading**, and, when relevant, **For the Family**. Remove the placeholder banner only after research, citation review, factual review, and editorial approval.
