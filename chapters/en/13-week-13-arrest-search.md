# Chapter 13 — Week 13: Arrest, Search & Prisoner Handling

> **Editorial sequence and research status.** Week 13 is this book's sequence, not a published HRCJTA schedule. Authorities were reviewed for the August 20, 2026 edition. This legal overview is not a field reference; current law, judicial decisions, instruction, and policy control.

## Opening

An arrest is not one isolated moment. Facts must support authority; the degree of restraint must match the legal basis; any search needs its own lawful justification and scope; property must be accounted for; medical needs remain real; transport and transfer create records. The paperwork later seen at a courthouse begins with decisions made much earlier.

## What This Week Is About

This chapter joins Chapter 2's constitutional framework, Chapter 6's evidence discipline, Chapter 8's custody responsibilities, Chapter 9's force law, and Chapter 3's factual reporting. It explains arrest, search, magistrate review, property, transport, and transfer conceptually. It gives no arrest mechanics, restraint method, concealed-contraband search method, or escape-prevention advice.

## What You Need to Understand

### Encounter, detention, and arrest are not interchangeable

A **consensual encounter** does not restrain a person's freedom to leave. An **investigative detention** requires reasonable suspicion and is limited in purpose and duration; *Terry* permits a protective frisk only under the distinct reasonable belief that the person is armed and dangerous. An **arrest** is a greater seizure requiring probable cause. Labels do not control: courts consider the circumstances and actual degree of restraint.

For example, asking a willing witness a question is not automatically detention. Briefly holding a person to investigate particular facts may be detention. Taking a person into custody to answer a criminal charge is arrest. Real cases can be less clear, so recruits must articulate objective facts and consult current law and policy.

### Virginia arrest authority is specific

An arrest warrant reflects a judicial officer's probable-cause determination and direction to take the named person into custody under its terms. Officers must confirm identity, validity, scope, and current procedures rather than assume that any database entry is self-executing.

Virginia Code § 19.2-81 authorizes warrantless arrests in specified circumstances, including categories of felonies and misdemeanors, but it is detailed and contains conditions, time limits, and offense-specific provisions. Section 19.2-81.3 adds duties and authorities for certain family-abuse, protective-order, stalking, and other matters. Neither provision should be reduced to “felony versus misdemeanor.” The facts, location, timing, officer's personal knowledge or probable cause, statutory category, and current policy matter.

For many misdemeanors, § 19.2-74 generally uses a summons and release process, subject to its stated exceptions and conditions. A summons is not “doing nothing”; it initiates a criminal process and commands appearance. Custodial arrest is not automatically preferable merely because it feels more decisive.

### Every search needs authority and scope

The Fourth Amendment generally requires reasonableness, and warrants are the normal constitutional preference. Recognized exceptions are fact-specific:

- **Consent** must be voluntary and stays within the consent given; authority to consent and withdrawal or limitation can matter.
- **Search incident to lawful arrest** has a lawful arrest as its premise and defined scope; *Robinson* does not erase limits, and *Riley* generally requires a warrant before searching digital information on a seized phone absent another exception.
- **Protective frisk** is the limited *Terry* doctrine, not a general evidence search.
- **Vehicle doctrines**, **exigent circumstances**, and **inventory functions** have separate legal predicates and limits. Inventory is an administrative caretaking process under standardized policy, not a pretext for investigation.

This guide deliberately does not teach where or how to find hidden items. The professional questions are: What authority exists? What is its lawful scope? What was done and found? How was integrity preserved?

### Virginia search-warrant process

Title 19.2, Chapter 5 governs Virginia search warrants. In broad form:

```text
Facts
   ↓
Probable cause
   ↓
Sworn affidavit / application
   ↓
Review by authorized judicial officer
   ↓
Warrant describing place/person and items
   ↓
Execution under law and policy
   ↓
Inventory, return, and documentation
```

Virginia Code § 19.2-54 addresses issuance upon probable cause supported by affidavit; §§ 19.2-56 and 19.2-57 address execution, return, and inventory requirements. Exact forms, time requirements, permissible means, special warrants, sealing, and exceptions require current instruction. A warrant is bounded judicial authority, not a general license to search.

### Magistrates are independent judicial officers

Virginia magistrates are part of the judicial system, not the police chain of command. Under Title 19.2 and the Office of the Executive Secretary's official manual, magistrates may perform functions that include issuing arrest and search warrants when legal requirements are met, making probable-cause determinations, conducting bail-related processes, and issuing commitment documentation. Their jurisdiction and authority are set by law.

An officer presents facts and legal grounds; the magistrate independently decides the question assigned by law. A denial is not permission to reshape facts. Bail, release, and commitment depend on governing statutes and the individual case, and some decisions belong to judges or other officials.

### Property categories must remain distinct

- **Personal property** belongs to the person and may accompany, be inventoried, released, or stored under procedure.
- **Evidence** has potential relevance to proof and requires preservation and chain-of-custody documentation.
- **Contraband** is property unlawful to possess under controlling law; the label requires a legal basis.
- **Safekeeping property** is held to protect it or account for it, not necessarily because it proves an offense.

One object may change classification as facts develop, but records must explain why. Receipts, inventories, seals, transfers, storage, and laboratory submission depend on agency and facility procedure. Never mix convenience with evidentiary purpose.

### Custody preserves dignity and medical responsibility

Confirm identity and destination; communicate in a language the person can understand using qualified language assistance when needed; observe injuries, behavior, consciousness, breathing, and complaints; ask about urgent medical issues and medications as trained; account for property; use restraints only under law, training, and policy; use installed vehicle safety restraints as required; monitor rather than punish; and document.

A person who is intoxicated, agitated, injured, ill, or reporting pain may need EMS or medical evaluation. Arrest does not make a medical complaint unimportant. A medical facility, jail, juvenile authority, magistrate process, or another agency may be the appropriate next location depending on age, charge, condition, warrant, jurisdiction, and law. No single pathway fits every case.

Transfer of custody is an accountability event. The receiving person or facility needs the information and property required by law and procedure, including known urgent risks. Record destination, time, condition, property, documentation delivered, and acceptance according to policy. Transport-security and restraint mechanics are outside this book.

### Document the decision, not a later defense

The record should explain objective facts supporting probable cause; warrant or warrantless authority; summons or custody decision; statements and their circumstances; searches and their legal basis and scope; items and exact disposition; force, if any; medical observations, complaints, aid, and refusals under procedure; transport; and transfer. Separate observation, reported information, inference, and later-learned facts. Do not write backward from the desired charge.

## What Training May Feel Like

Exercises may force repeated transitions: encounter to detention, detention to release or arrest, arrest to search, evidence to property record, and custody to medical or judicial process. The difficulty is not memorizing a single sequence. It is recognizing when authority changes and completing unglamorous documentation while treating a restrained person as a person.

## Key Concepts and Vocabulary

- **Arrest authority:** lawful basis to take a person into custody, derived from warrant or specific warrantless authority.
- **Probable cause:** facts and circumstances sufficient under current law for the relevant arrest or search decision.
- **Summons:** legal command to appear; in specified Virginia misdemeanor cases it may replace custodial process.
- **Search incident to arrest:** exception tied to a lawful arrest and its legally defined scope.
- **Booking:** administrative intake and record process; details vary by receiving facility.
- **Magistrate:** independent Virginia judicial officer with statutory functions, not an arm of the arresting agency.
- **Commitment:** judicial documentation authorizing detention under applicable law; not a synonym for every transfer.
- **Chain of custody:** documented possession and handling supporting evidence integrity.

## From the Classroom to the Street

**Illustrative, not tactical.** An officer develops facts supporting probable cause for an offense for which current Virginia law permits arrest. Before moving forward, the officer identifies whether a warrant, summons mandate, exception, or special statute applies. After lawful custody, each search and property decision is separately grounded and recorded. A reported medical problem changes the transport plan. This example intentionally supplies no physical procedure.

## From the Courthouse to the Academy

```text
Officer develops probable cause
        ↓
Arrest / summons / warrant process
        ↓
Magistrate or judicial review where applicable
        ↓
Custody / release decision
        ↓
Court docket
        ↓
Hearing / trial
```

Names, charge codes, warrants, returns, inventories, summonses, bond papers, commitment documents, property records, and officer reports familiar to a clerk originate in earlier decisions. A clean docket cannot repair an unsupported arrest, overbroad search, lost evidence, ignored medical need, or inaccurate report.

## What May Be Difficult

**Legal transitions:** restraint can transform an encounter. **Exceptions:** similar facts can lead to different authority. **Administration:** property and transfer records demand precision. **Dignity:** anger at alleged conduct cannot become punishment. **Uncertainty:** ask for legal or supervisory guidance rather than improvising authority.

## Questions to Think About

1. Is this consensual, a detention, or an arrest—and what facts support it?
2. Does Virginia law require or permit warrantless custody, a warrant, or a summons?
3. What independent authority and scope support any search?
4. Is an item evidence, contraband, personal property, or safekeeping property, and why?
5. What medical or communication need affects custody?
6. Who accepted custody and information, and how will that be documented?

## Week in Review

Custody is a legal and administrative continuum. Probable cause, a warrant, a statutory exception, or a summons rule must be applied precisely. Searches need their own authority and scope. Magistrates decide assigned questions independently. Property, medical care, transport, transfer, and truthful reporting are not secondary to arrest; they complete it.
