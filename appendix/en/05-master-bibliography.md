# Master Further-Reading Bibliography

This is a curated next-reading list, not a mechanical copy of chapter references. Official online materials are authoritative for the subject they govern and should be checked for updates. Access dates are **August 20, 2026**, except where noted.

## Virginia law and certification

- **Virginia Department of Criminal Justice Services. “Law Enforcement Training.”** https://www.dcjs.virginia.gov/law-enforcement/training. Current standards, performance outcomes, certification information, and adopted-future materials; recheck before an application or class.
- **Virginia Criminal Justice Services Board. “6VAC20-20, Rules Relating to Compulsory Minimum Training Standards for Law-Enforcement Officers.”** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title6/agency20/chapter20/. Controlling regulatory framework for foundational training; use the operative version.
- **Virginia Criminal Justice Services Board. “6VAC20-30, Rules Relating to Compulsory In-Service Training Standards.”** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title6/agency20/chapter30/. Continuing-training framework; requirements and reporting can change.
- **Virginia General Assembly. *Code of Virginia*, Title 15.2, Chapter 17.** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title15.2/chapter17/. Includes officer qualifications and training/certification provisions.
- **Hampton Roads Criminal Justice Training Academy. Official website.** https://www.hrcjta.com/. Use for information HRCJTA actually publishes; do not infer schedules or rules absent from the site.
- **James City County. “Police Jobs.”** https://www.jamescitycountyva.gov/765/Police-Jobs. Changeable recruiting information; compare with the current vacancy announcement.

## Constitutional policing and legal foundations

- **Library of Congress. *Constitution Annotated*.** https://constitution.congress.gov/. Maintained, official explanations linking constitutional text, doctrine, and cases.
- **Supreme Court of the United States. *Terry v. Ohio*, 392 U.S. 1 (1968); *Graham v. Connor*, 490 U.S. 386 (1989); *Tennessee v. Garner*, 471 U.S. 1 (1985); *Riley v. California*, 573 U.S. 373 (2014).** Read the opinions with current academy instruction and later law; summaries cannot replace fact-sensitive doctrine.
- **Supreme Court of Virginia. “Rules of the Supreme Court of Virginia.”** https://www.vacourts.gov/courts/scv/rulesofcourt.pdf. Current procedural and evidentiary rules; consult the live document rather than an old download.
- **Supreme Court of Virginia, Office of the Executive Secretary. *Magistrate Manual*.** https://www.vacourts.gov/courtadmin/aoc/mag/resources/manuals/magman/. Practical official account of the independent magistrate system.

## Communication and report writing

- **Federal Law Enforcement Training Centers. “Law Enforcement Training Resources.”** https://www.fletc.gov/training-resources. Federal instructional resources useful for professional communication context; not Virginia or JCCPD policy.
- **U.S. Department of Justice, Civil Rights Division. “Language Access.”** https://www.justice.gov/crt/language-access. Explains federal language-access principles and maintained resources.
- **Roediger, Henry L. III, and Jeffrey D. Karpicke. “Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention.” *Psychological Science* 17(3) (2006): 249–255.** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x. Useful evidence for retrieval-based study rather than rereading alone.

## Patrol and investigations

- **National Institute of Justice. *Crime Scene Investigation: A Guide for Law Enforcement*. 2013.** https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/crime-scene-investigation-guide-law-enforcement. General evidence-preservation and scene-management principles; local policy controls.
- **National Institute of Justice. *Eyewitness Evidence: A Guide for Law Enforcement*. 1999.** https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/eyewitness-evidence-guide-law-enforcement. Historically important guidance whose age and later research should be acknowledged.
- **Virginia General Assembly. *Code of Virginia*, Titles 18.2, 19.2, and 46.2.** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/. Primary criminal, procedure, and motor-vehicle law; use exact current sections rather than topic summaries.

## Victims, trauma, and crisis response

- **Virginia DCJS. “Victims Services.”** https://www.dcjs.virginia.gov/victims-services. Maintained Virginia program and resource locator.
- **Virginia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Services. “Crisis Services.”** https://dbhds.virginia.gov/behavioral-health/crisis-services/. Current system information; verify regional contacts and procedures.
- **U.S. Department of Justice, Office for Victims of Crime. *OVC Training and Technical Assistance Center*.** https://www.ovcttac.gov/. Victim-centered training resources; not a substitute for Virginia law.

## Ethics, de-escalation, and use of force

- **Police Executive Research Forum. *ICAT: Integrating Communications, Assessment, and Tactics Training Guide*. 2016.** https://www.policeforum.org/assets/icattrainingguide.pdf. A professional de-escalation framework; it is not Virginia law, DCJS standard, or JCCPD policy.
- **Police Executive Research Forum. *Transforming Police Recruit Training: 40 Guiding Principles*. 2022.** https://www.policeforum.org/assets/TransformingRecruitTraining.pdf. Recommendations for adult learning, judgment, and academy culture; distinguish recommendations from mandates.
- **Virginia General Assembly. *Code of Virginia* §§ 19.2-83.3–19.2-83.5.** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title19.2/chapter7/. Current Virginia force, intervention, and reporting provisions; read exact text.

## Firearms safety and professional judgment

- **Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives. “Safety and Security for Firearm Owners.”** https://www.atf.gov/firearms/safety-and-security-firearm-owners. General secure-access principles; police policy and supervised instruction control duty equipment.
- **National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health. *Reducing Exposure to Lead and Noise at Outdoor Firing Ranges*. DHHS (NIOSH) Publication 2011-104, 2010.** https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2011-104/. Occupational-health perspective for range exposure.

## Emergency driving

- **Virginia General Assembly. *Code of Virginia* § 46.2-920.** https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title46.2/chapter8/section46.2-920/. Primary statement of conditional emergency-vehicle exemptions and continuing safety obligations.
- **National Highway Traffic Safety Administration. “First Responder Safety.”** https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/first-responder-safety. Maintained roadway-risk resources; not pursuit authorization or tactics.

## First aid and emergency response

- **American Heart Association. “CPR and ECC Guidelines.”** https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines. Current science locator; only an approved hands-on course can provide certification.
- **American College of Surgeons. “STOP THE BLEED.”** https://www.stopthebleed.org/. Public training locator; use formal instruction rather than text alone.
- **Virginia Department of Health. “Emergency Medical Services” and “Naloxone.”** https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/emergency-medical-services/ and https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/epidemiology/naloxone/. Virginia program information; protocols and programs change.
- **Occupational Safety and Health Administration. “Bloodborne Pathogens and Needlestick Prevention.”** https://www.osha.gov/bloodborne-pathogens. Workplace exposure-prevention and follow-up framework.

## Field training and professional development

- **Dunlosky, John, et al. “Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques.” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1) (2013): 4–58.** https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266. Evidence-based comparison of study methods.
- **National Institute of Justice. *Field Training for Police Officers: The State of the Art*. 1987.** https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/105574NCJRS.pdf. Historically useful for understanding field training's development, but not a current Virginia or JCCPD model.

## Officer wellness and police families

- **U.S. Department of Justice, COPS Office. *Law Enforcement Mental Health and Wellness Act: Report to Congress*. 2019.** https://cops.usdoj.gov/RIC/Publications/cops-p370-pub.pdf. National discussion of wellness programs, peer support, and families; confirm local services and confidentiality.
- **U.S. Department of Justice, COPS Office. “Officer Safety and Wellness Resources.”** https://cops.usdoj.gov/officersafetyandwellness. Maintained collection for agencies, officers, and families.
- **National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health. “Work Schedules: Shift Work and Long Hours.”** https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/topics/workschedules/. General occupational-health guidance; not an agency schedule or individual medical plan.
- **Caldwell, John A., et al. “Fatigue and Its Management in the Workplace.” *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 96 (2019): 272–289.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.10.024. Research synthesis for understanding fatigue as a safety issue.

## Curation decisions

This list favors primary law, official agencies, transparent professional organizations, and research syntheses. It excludes commercial test-prep sites, tactical manuals, unsourced blogs, and books whose fame exceeds their fit with a safety-first, constitutional, victim-aware, accountable approach. Older NIJ items remain only where their historical value is explicit.
