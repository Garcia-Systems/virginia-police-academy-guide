# Physical Preparation Guide

> **General preparation—not an official HRCJTA workout plan.** This moderate framework does not reproduce or guarantee passage of any academy, DCJS, or JCCPD standard. Obtain the current written standard and effective date from the organizations that control your cohort.

## Know the official standard first

DCJS's compulsory-minimum materials include physical-wellness and performance content, but the public records available to this project did not establish a single statewide applicant fitness cutoff. No current HRCJTA entrance/graduation score table or JCCPD hiring fitness score was verified from a public authoritative source. Another academy's test is not a substitute.

Ask the recruiter or academy, in writing if possible:

1. Is there an entrance screen, graduation standard, or both?
2. What events, form, scoring, rest, technique, and retest rules apply?
3. Which version and effective date govern this cohort, including January 1, 2027 changes?
4. Is medical documentation or clearance required?

## Safety note

People with significant medical conditions, concerning symptoms, a previous major injury, pregnancy, or another health concern should seek appropriate professional medical guidance before strenuous training. Stop and seek prompt help for alarming symptoms. This guide does not diagnose injury or prescribe individual care.

## Build a base, not a boot camp

### Aerobic work

Begin at a level that permits consistent training. Brisk walking, walk/jog intervals, easy running, cycling, or another appropriate low-impact activity can build capacity. Add time or intensity gradually rather than increasing everything at once. Most sessions should feel controlled enough to speak in short sentences.

### Strength

Two nonconsecutive sessions can cover broad patterns: a squat or sit-to-stand, a hip hinge, a push, a pull, a step or carry, and trunk stability. Bodyweight, bands, or manageable external resistance are all options. Use controlled form, leave repetitions in reserve, and learn unfamiliar movements from a qualified professional. Do not imitate defensive tactics at home.

### Mobility

Use brief, comfortable movement through the joints needed for walking, running, squatting, reaching, and getting up from the ground. Warm up dynamically before exertion; reserve longer relaxed stretching for afterward or a separate session. Pain is not a flexibility target.

### Recovery, hydration, and footwear

- Protect regular sleep; learning and physical adaptation both depend on recovery.
- Drink normally and adjust for weather and exertion. Never use deliberate dehydration.
- Eat regular, adequate meals rather than crash dieting.
- Use activity-appropriate shoes that fit now; introduce new footwear before a demanding day.
- Schedule at least one easier or rest day each week.

### Injury prevention

Track duration, effort, surface, and symptoms. Ordinary effort and mild transient soreness differ from sharp pain, altered gait, persistent swelling, fainting, chest pain, or worsening symptoms. Do not hide an injury to preserve a training streak. Early assessment and an adjusted plan are usually more useful than punishment workouts.

## Preparing for academy mornings

Without pretending to know HRCJTA's daily schedule, practice a stable routine: move bedtime and waking time gradually, pack clothing and food the night before, allow a realistic commute buffer, and test breakfast/hydration choices during ordinary training—not on an important assessment morning.

## A moderate four-to-eight-week framework

Repeat the weekly pattern for four weeks if already active, or build toward it over as many as eight. Session length and mode must match the individual's starting point.

| Day | Focus | Moderate purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Easy aerobic + brief mobility | Build repeatable volume. |
| Tuesday | Full-body strength | Practice controlled foundational patterns. |
| Wednesday | Easy aerobic or walk/jog | Add capacity without racing. |
| Thursday | Mobility/recovery | Easy movement, sleep, and symptom check. |
| Friday | Full-body strength | Repeat patterns; avoid maximal lifting. |
| Saturday | Longer easy aerobic session | Progress time gradually, not all variables. |
| Sunday | Rest or very easy mobility | Absorb the week and plan the next. |

**Progression:** during weeks 1–2, establish comfortable consistency. During weeks 3–4, add a small amount of time to one or two sessions if recovery is good. During weeks 5–6, maintain frequency and cautiously make one aerobic session somewhat more purposeful. During weeks 7–8, avoid a last-minute surge; practice the routine and arrive recovered. Reduce the plan when work, illness, pain, heat, or sleep loss makes the planned load unwise.

## What this plan intentionally excludes

No excessive mileage, maximal lifting, punishment exercise, sleep restriction, dehydration, weighted forced marching, unsupervised obstacle work, firearms handling, driving drills, or physical-control practice belongs here. Preparation should increase readiness without creating a preventable injury.
