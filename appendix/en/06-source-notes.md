# Source and Research Notes

## What this appendix tells the reader

This book is an educational guide built from public sources, not an academy syllabus or agency manual. These notes explain how claims were selected, dated, localized, translated, and limited. The shared source register and research log preserve the project's working audit trail.

> **Research date: August 20, 2026. Readers should verify current official requirements before relying on the book for a real application, academy class, or legal decision.**

## Source hierarchy

The project generally preferred sources in this order:

1. **Virginia DCJS** for administered training and certification information;
2. **Code of Virginia** for enacted statutory authority and duties;
3. **Virginia Administrative Code** for operative board regulations;
4. **HRCJTA** for facts the academy itself publishes;
5. **James City County / James City County Police** for local recruiting and agency facts;
6. **Virginia courts** for state judicial rules, opinions, and magistrate materials;
7. **authoritative state and federal agencies** for health, safety, victims, research, and federal law;
8. **academic and professional sources** for learning, human performance, and recommended practice; and
9. **carefully selected secondary literature** for context.

Primary sources are favored because a blog or training vendor cannot create a Virginia legal requirement, establish JCCPD policy, or reveal an unpublished HRCJTA practice. Secondary sources can explain or contextualize, but the manuscript labels recommendations and does not promote them into mandates.

## Claim labels and local boundaries

During drafting, a factual statement was treated as one of: Virginia law/regulation; DCJS standard; HRCJTA-published information; James City County/JCCPD-published information; generally accepted professional concept; or this book's editorial organization. That distinction prevents three common errors: presenting general practice as local policy, presenting a local job advertisement as statewide law, and presenting this book's organization as an academy calendar.

Public HRCJTA and James City County information did not disclose every schedule, score, assessment rule, physical cutoff, field-training model, probation term, shift pattern, or internal policy. Where authoritative public evidence was unavailable, the book avoids guessing, uses clearly labeled general principles where useful, and states the limitation.

## The 19-week structure is editorial

> **The numbered 19-week sequence is an editorial learning framework unless a particular HRCJTA scheduling statement is explicitly sourced.**

It groups subjects into a comprehensible journey. It does not claim that HRCJTA teaches a subject in that week, uses those chapter titles, follows that order, or publishes the daily scenarios described as illustrations.

## Currency and effective dates

Law, regulations, standards, web pages, and policy can change. Research records therefore capture the authority, exact title where available, URL or publication data, source/revision date when shown, access date, and a note about scope. Statutory references point readers to the official live code; summaries repeatedly direct readers to exact current text.

The book separates a requirement effective on the research date from a later adopted requirement. For initial training, it labels the then-current August 20, 2026 framework separately from DCJS materials identified for **January 1, 2027** implementation. It does not invent a transition rule for a cohort spanning that date. DCJS and the academy must identify the controlling version.

The final freshness attempt could not reopen official sites because the research environment's HTTPS tunnel returned 403 responses. That limitation is recorded rather than hidden. Existing authoritative records were retained, and exact titles, revision stamps, transition guidance, and live status are on the final prepublication recheck list.

## Legal verification

Legal concepts were checked against the Constitution, official United States Reports or official court materials, the Code of Virginia, Virginia regulations, and official court resources. Holdings are described narrowly. No glossary definition or chapter summary resolves a real case: facts, later decisions, statutory amendments, jurisdiction, and agency legal guidance matter.

**This book provides educational legal information, not legal advice.** It cannot replace academy instruction, agency policy, counsel, current statutes, regulations, or case law.

## Training and safety boundary

Written description cannot replace supervised, qualified instruction in defensive tactics, firearms, emergency vehicle operation, CPR/AED, first aid, restraints, or tactical decision-making. The manuscript emphasizes authority, judgment, safety, de-escalation, communication, documentation, and accountability and intentionally omits mechanics that would be unsafe to learn from prose.

The physical-preparation appendix is moderate general conditioning, not a medical plan or an official HRCJTA/JCCPD workout. No unverified score is borrowed from another academy.

## Spanish adaptation methodology

English is the canonical research language because Virginia law, academy instruction, and agency work operate primarily in English. Spanish is a full comprehension-focused adaptation, not an independent legal edition. It preserves official English organization names, case names, important professional terms, and workplace acronyms such as FTO, EMS, CPR, AED, EAP, ECO, and TDO.

The terminology table in `docs/TRANSLATION_GUIDE.md` controls recurring pairs. Context-dependent choices are explained rather than forced: *search* may be **registro** in constitutional-policing context; **custodia** has police, emergency, and family meanings; **citation** and **summons** are not collapsed into one Spanish word. Changes to English factual claims require a parity review in Spanish.

## Link and source audit status

The source register classifies source purpose and age. Superseded material should be replaced; moved material should point to the authoritative new location; unavailable material should not establish a present rule; archived material may remain only when clearly historical. The bibliography deliberately labels older NIJ field-training, eyewitness, and restraint-risk publications as historical rather than current local procedure.

Because live network validation was blocked in this environment, the audit is **content-complete but connectivity-incomplete**. The following high-impact locators require one final ordinary-network check: DCJS current and 2027 standards; 6VAC20-20; 6VAC20-30; HRCJTA basic-academy pages; JCCPD Police Jobs; certification-examination directions; and the heavily cited Code sections in Titles 15.2, 19.2, 37.2, and 46.2.

## What the book does not claim

- that admission, graduation, certification, field training, probation, or employment is guaranteed;
- that one agency's sequence applies statewide;
- that the 19 editorial weeks reproduce HRCJTA's calendar;
- that public recruiting text reveals internal JCCPD policy;
- that a written explanation teaches a physical or tactical skill;
- that a legal summary decides an actual event; or
- that a source current on August 20, 2026 will remain current.

Responsible use means treating the book as preparation for better questions, not as authority to bypass the current instructor, supervisor, policy, statute, regulation, medical professional, or court.
